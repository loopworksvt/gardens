Catmint

Nepeta x faassenii

perenniallate spring, repeating after shearing

A drought-tolerant perennial with aromatic gray-green foliage and spikes of lavender-blue flowers that bloom repeatedly throughout the growing season. The soft, mounded habit and long flowering period make it excellent for edging paths or filling in perennial borders. Cats are attracted to some varieties, and the plant is deer resistant while being beloved by bees and butterflies.

Catmint (Nepeta) is a hardy, drought-tolerant perennial herb known for its aromatic gray-green foliage and prolific lavender-blue flower spikes that provide continuous blooms from late spring through fall. This low-maintenance plant serves multiple garden functions as an excellent border edging, pollinator magnet, and natural pest deterrent while being particularly attractive to cats due to its nepetalactone compounds.

Care

Summarized from the sources listed below (web research, 2026-11-02). Where sources disagree, the page says so.

What helps it thrive

Full sun and good drainage are the main needs. Not fussy about soil type. Drought tolerant once established, though Wisconsin notes it does better when well watered.

Growing season

Flowers in late spring. Cutting back by one third to one half (some sources say hard, to a few inches) after the first flush fades tidies the foliage and encourages a second flush; shearing in spring gives a more compact habit. Many hybrids are sterile and do not reseed.

Fall tasks

Not covered by the sources found. Sources covered post-bloom cutting back but not a fall routine.

Propagation

Division in spring, or softwood cuttings in summer (June-July): non-flowering side shoots about 3 inches long root in around 4 weeks. The RHS also lists semi-ripe cuttings in early autumn for some species.

Dividing

Seldom needed (Illinois). When wanted, divide in spring (the RHS says spring or autumn for 'Kit Cat'). Hybrid catmint does not take over the bed the way many mints do.

Research notes

Researched 2026-05-12.

Research (May 12, 2026)
Catmint belongs to the genus Nepeta in the mint family (Lamiaceae) and encompasses over 250 species of aromatic perennial herbs. The most commonly cultivated garden varieties, such as Nepeta × faassenii and Nepeta racemosa, are prized for their exceptional drought tolerance and ability to thrive in poor soils with minimal care. The plant's distinctive gray-green foliage releases a pleasant, minty fragrance when brushed or crushed, while its tubular flowers arranged in terminal spikes provide nectar-rich blooms that attract bees, butterflies, and other beneficial pollinators throughout the growing season.

The compound nepetalactone found in catmint leaves creates the euphoric response in domestic cats, though this reaction varies among individual animals and plant varieties. Garden varieties like 'Walker's Low' and 'Six Hills Giant' have been specifically bred for ornamental purposes, offering improved flower production and more compact growth habits. Beyond its ornamental value, catmint serves as an effective companion plant, naturally repelling rodents, aphids, and other garden pests while remaining unpalatable to deer. The plant's ability to self-seed and spread through underground rhizomes makes it valuable for naturalizing in wildflower gardens or creating low-maintenance ground cover in challenging growing conditions.
