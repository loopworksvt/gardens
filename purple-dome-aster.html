Purple Dome Aster

Symphyotrichum novae-angliae 'Purple Dome'

perennialmid-August to October

A compact fall-blooming perennial that forms a perfect mounded shape covered in masses of small purple daisy-like flowers. This native aster blooms from late summer through fall, providing crucial nectar for butterflies and bees when few other flowers are available. The dense, disease-resistant foliage stays attractive all season without the need for staking or pinching.

Purple Dome Aster (Symphyotrichum novae-angliae 'Purple Dome') is a compact cultivar of New England Aster that forms a perfectly rounded mound 18-24 inches tall and wide, covered in masses of small purple flowers from late summer through fall. This native perennial is highly valued for its late-season nectar production, supporting migrating monarchs and other pollinators when few other flowers remain blooming.

Care

Summarized from the sources listed below (web research, 2026-11-02). Where sources disagree, the page says so.

What helps it thrive

Sun or partial shade in fertile, moist but well-drained soil, preferably neutral to alkaline. Will not tolerate winter waterlogging. Good airflow limits leaf disease, and the plant is prone to powdery mildew in dry soil. Grows 18-24 inches tall and up to 3 feet wide.

Growing season

Blooms from mid-August into early October, covering the plant in purple. Pinching stems back by a third to a half once or twice before the end of June (some sources say mid-July) controls height, makes it bushier and may remove the need for staking, but delays flowering.

Fall tasks

Mulch in autumn. Cut old flowered stems back to the ground in late autumn.

Propagation

Division is the best way to keep the cultivar true. Softwood cuttings can be taken in late spring. Seed is less reliable for a cultivar; it can be sown outside in fall or cold-stratified.

Dividing

Every 2-3 years (RHS says every third year; Wisconsin says every 2-3 years; White Flower Farm says every year or two). Divide in early spring, around March or April, by sectioning off stems with their attached roots.

Research notes

Researched 2026-05-12.

Research (May 12, 2026)
Purple Dome Aster represents one of the most successful compact selections of the native New England Aster, bred specifically to maintain the species' exceptional pollinator value while eliminating common garden problems like tall, floppy growth and powdery mildew susceptibility. The cultivar produces hundreds of small, violet-purple daisy flowers with bright yellow centers that bloom reliably from August through October, creating a spectacular fall display that coincides perfectly with monarch butterfly migration patterns. Research has shown that native asters like Purple Dome support significantly more beneficial insects than non-native alternatives, with studies documenting over 100 species of butterflies and moths whose caterpillars feed on Symphyotrichum species. The plant's compact 18-24 inch mounded form requires no staking, deadheading, or pinching to maintain its shape, making it exceptionally low-maintenance compared to the straight species. Its dense, small leaves remain healthy and attractive throughout the growing season, showing excellent resistance to the powdery mildew that often plagues other asters in humid conditions. Purple Dome Aster thrives in full sun and adapts to various soil conditions, from clay to sandy soils, though it performs best with moderate moisture and good drainage.
