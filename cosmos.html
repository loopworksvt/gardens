Cosmos

Cosmos bipinnatus

annualabout 7 weeks from sowing to first frost

Cosmos (Cosmos bipinnatus and related species) are fast-growing, easy-care annual or perennial flowering plants prized for their daisy-like blooms in pink, red, white, and orange hues. They thrive in full sun with well-drained, low-fertility soil and are excellent for attracting pollinators and serving as companion plants in the garden.

Care

What helps it thrive

At least half a day of direct sun in well-drained soil. Rich soil favors leaves over flowers, and over-fertilizing or over-watering gives fewer blooms. Tall varieties may need support against wind and storms.

Growing season

Sow direct once the soil is warm (at least 65F), no more than 1/4 inch deep; germination takes 7-21 days. Indoors, start 4-6 weeks before the last frost. First bloom comes about 7 weeks after sowing and continues until the first fall frost. Annuals do not survive winter.

Fall tasks

Seed heads can be left to blow away in fall to self-sow, which brings volunteers next year; remove spent flowers earlier if you want to keep plants where you put them.

Propagation

Seed. It is among the easiest seeds to harvest: wait until the heads are brown and dry (never harvest when wet), spread seeds on paper to dry for about a week, then store in an envelope or jar.

Dividing

Not applicable; annual grown from seed.

Research (Aug 5, 2026)
Cosmos is a genus of flowering plants in the family Asteraceae, native to Mexico and Central America, with Cosmos bipinnatus (garden cosmos) and Cosmos sulphureus (sulfur cosmos) being the most widely cultivated species. The plants produce feathery, fern-like foliage and vibrant daisy-like flowers that bloom from early summer through frost, making them one of the longest-blooming annuals available to gardeners. Their rapid growth habit and prolific self-seeding mean they can naturalize in a garden over successive seasons with minimal effort.

One of the most appealing characteristics of cosmos is their preference for poor to average soil — overly rich or fertilized soil encourages lush foliage at the expense of flowers. They are drought-tolerant once established and require only occasional watering, making them ideal for low-maintenance garden designs. Deadheading spent blooms prolongs flowering, though leaving some seed heads at the end of season encourages self-sowing for the following year. Plants grow anywhere from 18 inches to 6 feet tall depending on variety, so staking may be needed in windy locations.

Cosmos are highly valued as companion plants because their open, nectar-rich flowers attract beneficial insects including bees, butterflies, lacewings, and parasitic wasps that help control garden pests. They are commonly interplanted with vegetables and herbs to support biodiversity and integrated pest management. Popular cultivars include 'Sensation Mix,' 'Sonata,' and 'Versailles,' each varying in height, color range, and bloom size. Direct sowing seeds after last frost is the preferred planting method, as cosmos do not transplant well due to their delicate taproots.
