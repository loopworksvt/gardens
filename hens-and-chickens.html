Hens and Chickens

Sempervivum tectorum

perennialsummer; the flowering rosette dies afterward

Evergreen succulent that forms rosettes ('hens') and spreads by offsets ('chicks'). The mother rosette flowers after two or more years and then dies, and its offsets take over.

Care

Summarized from the sources listed below (web research, 2026-11-02). Where sources disagree, the page says so.

What helps it thrive

Full sun and very sharp drainage; sandy or gravelly soil suits it. Overwatering and constantly wet conditions cause rot. NC State suggests some afternoon shade in extremely hot sun.

Growing season

Evergreen, needing little in the growing season. The mother rosette flowers after two or more years and then dies, but offsets are already in place to replace it.

Fall tasks

Generally needs no winter protection, but may rot if it stays too wet in cold months (RHS: protect from excessive winter wet). Rake up fallen leaves in autumn to limit wet debris, and move containers to a sheltered spot so pots do not freeze and crack.

Propagation

Offsets are quick and reliable; seed is slow and often not true to type. Wait until the chicks have rooted, separate them with a knife or pruners keeping roots as intact as possible, and plant promptly. For unrooted mini-rosettes, snip the connecting stolon close to the rosette; rooting takes a few weeks in gritty compost out of direct sun.

Dividing

Split vigorous plants in spring; container plants every 2-3 years in spring (RHS). The Almanac says to divide as soon as the chicks have rooted rather than waiting for a season, and the RHS gives spring to early summer for removing offsets.
