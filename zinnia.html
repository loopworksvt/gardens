Zinnia elegans

annualsummer to first hard frost

Bright annual flower available in nearly every color except blue, with forms ranging from tiny pompons to dinner-plate sized blooms. These heat-loving plants bloom continuously from summer until frost with minimal care, making them perfect for cutting gardens and butterfly habitats. Easy to grow from seed and excellent for beginning gardeners, zinnias attract beneficial insects while providing long-lasting cut flowers.

Zinnias are vibrant annual flowers native to Mexico that have become one of the most popular garden plants worldwide due to their exceptional heat tolerance and continuous blooming from summer until frost. These versatile flowers come in an extraordinary range of colors, sizes, and forms, making them invaluable for both ornamental gardens and as cut flowers while simultaneously supporting pollinators and beneficial insects.

Care

Summarized from the sources listed below (web research, 2026-11-02). Where sources disagree, the page says so.

What helps it thrive

Full sun (6-8 hours or more) and good drainage; soil rich in organic matter is ideal. Space about 12 inches apart in humid areas for airflow, and water at the base rather than overhead to reduce mildew.

Growing season

Direct sow outdoors after the last frost once the soil is warm (about 70F), 1/4 inch deep; seedlings appear in 4-7 days. Succession sowing every few weeks through early summer extends bloom. Plants die with the first hard frost. Zinnias dislike root disturbance; if started indoors, sow 4-6 weeks early and transplant carefully.

Fall tasks

A true annual, finished by hard frost. The sources gave no cleanup steps. To save seed, leave a few blooms until they are brown and fully dry.

Propagation

Seed only. Dry the blossoms in a paper bag until the seeds shake loose, then store in a paper envelope somewhere cool, dark and dry until spring. Hybrid or mixed seed may not come true.

Dividing

Not applicable; zinnia is an annual grown from seed.

Research notes

Researched 2026-05-12.

Research (May 12, 2026)
Zinnias (Zinnia elegans) are warm-season annuals belonging to the Asteraceae family, originally native to scrublands of Mexico but now cultivated globally. These remarkable flowers demonstrate exceptional adaptability to hot, dry conditions and poor soils, making them ideal for xerophytic gardening and low-maintenance landscapes. The genus encompasses over 20 species, with Z. elegans being the most commonly grown, offering an unprecedented color palette that includes virtually every hue except true blue, from soft pastels to vibrant oranges, reds, and purples.

The morphological diversity of zinnias is equally impressive, ranging from tiny 'Thumbelina' varieties measuring just 6 inches tall with small pompom-like flowers, to towering 'State Fair Mix' cultivars reaching 3-4 feet with blooms spanning 5-6 inches across. This size versatility allows gardeners to incorporate zinnias into various design schemes, from compact container gardens to expansive cutting gardens. Their daisy-like composite flowers consist of colorful ray petals surrounding a central disc, attracting numerous pollinators including butterflies, bees, and beneficial insects that provide natural pest control.

From a horticultural perspective, zinnias are exceptionally beginner-friendly, germinating readily from direct-sown seed in warm soil and requiring minimal care once established. They thrive in full sun with well-draining soil and demonstrate remarkable drought tolerance once mature. Their prolific blooming habit and excellent vase life make them premier cut flowers, while their ability to self-seed ensures natural succession in informal garden settings.
