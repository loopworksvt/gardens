Siberian Squill

Scilla siberica

bulbearly spring

Early spring bulb that produces carpets of intense blue bell-shaped flowers before most other plants emerge from winter dormancy. The narrow, strap-like leaves appear with the flowers and fade away by late spring, allowing the bulbs to naturalize under deciduous trees. Extremely hardy and reliable, these bulbs multiply freely to create stunning blue drifts that herald the arrival of spring.

Siberian Squill (Scilla siberica) is a small spring-blooming bulb that produces intense blue bell-shaped flowers in early spring, often while snow is still present. These hardy bulbs naturalize readily under deciduous trees, forming expansive blue carpets that multiply year after year with minimal care.

Care

Summarized from the sources listed below (web research, 2026-11-02). Where sources disagree, the page says so.

What helps it thrive

Full sun to part shade in well-drained soil. Very cold hardy and low maintenance. It naturalizes readily by self-seeding and offsets; the University of Minnesota lists it as potentially invasive, so think before planting near natural areas. NC State flags it as poisonous, so keep it away from children and pets.

Growing season

Blooms in early spring. Plant bulbs in early to mid fall.

Fall tasks

This is the planting season: set bulbs 2-3 inches deep (or about three times the bulb's height), 1-2 inches apart for a dense carpet.

Propagation

Offsets lifted in summer, or seed sown in pots in a cold frame when ripe, kept shaded and not allowed to dry out. It also spreads on its own by seed and offsets.

Dividing

The sources gave no detailed steps. The RHS says remove offsets in summer; lift them while the foliage is gone and replant promptly.

Research notes

Researched 2026-05-12.

Research (May 12, 2026)
Siberian Squill is one of the earliest and most reliable spring bulbs, typically blooming in March or April depending on climate zone. The small bulbs produce clusters of 3-5 nodding, bell-shaped flowers in brilliant blue, though white and pink varieties exist. Each bulb sends up multiple flower stems alongside narrow, strap-like leaves that emerge simultaneously with the blooms. The foliage completes its photosynthesis cycle and dies back by late spring, making this an ideal companion for deciduous trees and shrubs where it can receive full sun during its growing period but tolerate summer shade.

These bulbs are exceptionally hardy, surviving temperatures well below freezing and thriving in USDA zones 2-8. They prefer well-drained soil but are adaptable to various conditions, including clay and sandy soils. Siberian Squill naturalizes aggressively through both seed dispersal and bulb division, making it excellent for large-scale plantings but potentially invasive in some regions. The bulbs require a cold winter period for proper flower development and perform best when planted in fall at a depth of 3-4 inches. Once established, they require virtually no maintenance and will multiply into stunning blue drifts that can persist for decades.
