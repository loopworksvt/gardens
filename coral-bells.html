Coral Bells

Heuchera

perennialzones 4-9late spring to summer

A versatile perennial prized for its colorful foliage ranging from lime green to deep burgundy, bronze, and silver. Delicate spikes of small bell-shaped flowers rise above the mounded leaves in late spring to early summer. Excellent for adding year-round color to shady areas and works beautifully in containers or as ground cover.

Coral Bells (Heuchera) are versatile North American native perennials prized for their colorful, year-round foliage and delicate bell-shaped flower spikes. Flower scapes should be cut back to the base after blooming finishes in early to midsummer to keep the plant tidy and encourage continued foliage energy.

Care

Summarized from the sources listed below (web research, 2026-11-02). Where sources disagree, the page says so.

What helps it thrive

Part shade, with morning sun and afternoon shade, is ideal; vividly colored foliage bleaches in strong sun. Needs moist but well-drained soil rich in organic matter. Winter drainage matters most, since plants perish if the bed stays wet. Shallow roots need regular water, about an inch a week without rain.

Growing season

Hardy roughly zones 4-9. Foliage is the main show, and flower spikes come in late spring to summer. Best planted in spring, or in early fall at least 6 weeks before the ground freezes.

Fall tasks

Do not cut back in fall; remove dead leaves in spring. After the ground freezes, mulch 2-4 inches (straw or shredded leaves) to stop frost heaving, which can push shallow-rooted plants out of the soil.

Propagation

Easily propagated by division or leaf cuttings, or from seed. Seed needs a cold period of about 6 weeks and is sown no deeper than 1/8 inch; seedlings flower the second year.

Dividing

Every 3-4 years, because the center turns woody and thin. Sources conflict on timing: White Flower Farm says spring only (fall plants need a full season to establish), while the RHS, NC State and the Lady Bird Johnson Wildflower Center say autumn works. In a cold zone, spring is the safer choice. Dig the clump, shake off loose soil, pull or cut it into sections each with healthy roots and a crown, discard the woody center, replant with the crown at ground level and water well.

Research (May 12, 2026)
Coral Bells, scientifically known as Heuchera, represent one of the most popular foliage plants in modern gardening due to their remarkable leaf color diversity and adaptability. Native to North America, these herbaceous perennials have been extensively hybridized to produce cultivars with leaves in shades of purple, red, orange, yellow, green, and silver, often with intricate veining or marbled patterns. The plants typically form neat mounds 8-12 inches tall and wide, making them ideal for edging, mass plantings, or mixed borders.

While grown primarily for their foliage, Coral Bells produce airy panicles of small, bell-shaped flowers in late spring through early summer. These flower spikes can reach 12-24 inches tall and come in colors ranging from white and pink to red and coral, attracting hummingbirds and other pollinators. The plants prefer partial shade to full shade conditions, though some newer varieties tolerate more sun, and they thrive in well-draining, slightly acidic to neutral soil.

Coral Bells have become indispensable in contemporary landscape design for their ability to provide consistent color throughout the growing season. They work exceptionally well in container gardens, rock gardens, and as companions to hostas, ferns, and other shade perennials. Their compact size and diverse color palette make them particularly valuable for creating sophisticated color combinations and adding texture to shaded garden spaces where flowering plants may struggle.

Research (Aug 5, 2026)
Coral Bells (Heuchera spp.) are herbaceous to semi-evergreen perennials native to North America, belonging to the family Saxifragaceae. They are among the most hybridized ornamental perennials, with modern cultivars offering an extraordinary range of foliage colors including deep burgundy, bronze, caramel, lime green, silver, and near-black. Popular cultivars include 'Palace Purple,' 'Caramel,' 'Obsidian,' and 'Lime Rickey.' Plants form low, mounded rosettes typically 8–18 inches tall, with flower scapes rising 12–24 inches above the foliage in late spring to early summer.

Regarding deadheading and cutting back flowers: once the delicate, airy flower spikes have finished blooming — generally by midsummer — they should be cut back at the base of the scape using clean scissors or pruners. This prevents the plant from expending energy on seed production and keeps the display focused on the ornamental foliage. If you wish to allow some self-seeding or enjoy the extended airy texture, you may leave scapes standing briefly, but prompt removal encourages a tidier mound. In late fall or early spring, any tattered or frost-damaged outer leaves can be gently removed to refresh the plant's appearance.

Coral Bells thrive in partial to full shade, though some modern hybrids tolerate more sun, especially in cooler climates. They prefer well-draining, humus-rich soil with consistent moisture but are intolerant of waterlogged conditions. Dividing clumps every 3–4 years prevents the crown from heaving out of the soil, a common issue. They are excellent in containers, woodland gardens, and mixed borders, pairing beautifully with hostas, ferns, and astilbes.
