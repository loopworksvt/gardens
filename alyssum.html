Alyssum

Lobularia maritima

annualspring to fall; slows in summer heat

Low-growing annual that forms carpets of tiny four-petaled flowers in white, pink, or purple with a sweet honey fragrance that attracts beneficial insects. The small, narrow leaves are nearly hidden beneath the profusion of blooms from spring until frost in cooler climates. Excellent for edging, rock gardens, and spilling over container edges, alyssum thrives in cool weather and may slow down during hot summers.

Alyssum is a hardy, low-growing annual flower that produces dense carpets of tiny, fragrant blooms in white, pink, or purple from spring until frost. This cool-weather loving plant is prized for its sweet honey scent that attracts beneficial insects and its versatility in edging, rock gardens, and containers.

Care

Summarized from the sources listed below (web research, 2026-11-02). Where sources disagree, the page says so.

What helps it thrive

Full sun in cool climates, partial shade in hot ones, in average well-drained soil with medium moisture. Overly fertile soil promotes foliage over flowers. Mature plants tolerate a light frost; young plants do not.

Growing season

Flowering slows and plants look ragged in summer heat, then revive with cooler weather and last well into fall. Seed-grown plants may stop flowering in the heat; sterile hybrids hold up better. Shear back by half in the dog days with light watering and fertilizing to help recovery. Flowers start 6-8 weeks from sowing.

Fall tasks

Sow seed in August or September for fall bloom. Established plants carry on into fall until hard frost.

Propagation

Seed. Barely cover it, since it needs light to germinate. Sow outdoors 2-3 weeks before the last frost once the soil has thawed, or indoors 8-10 weeks before the last frost.

Dividing

Not applicable; alyssum is an annual grown from seed.

Research notes

Researched 2026-05-12.

Research (May 12, 2026)
Alyssum (Lobularia maritima), commonly known as sweet alyssum, is a diminutive annual flower that has become indispensable in cool-season gardening. The plant typically grows only 4-6 inches tall but spreads 6-12 inches wide, creating dense mats of tiny four-petaled flowers that nearly obscure its narrow, gray-green foliage. Its distinctive sweet honey fragrance serves a dual purpose, delighting gardeners while attracting beneficial insects such as hoverflies, parasitic wasps, and other pollinators that help control garden pests naturally.

The plant's remarkable cold tolerance sets it apart from many other annuals, as it can withstand light frosts and continues blooming well into fall in temperate climates. Alyssum performs best in cool weather between 60-75°F and may cease flowering or become leggy during hot summer periods, though it often rebounds with cooler fall temperatures. Its adaptability to various soil conditions, from sandy to clay, and its tolerance for both full sun and partial shade make it an excellent choice for challenging garden situations.

Gardeners particularly value alyssum for its versatility in landscape design. Its cascading growth habit makes it ideal for softening hard edges in pathways, spilling gracefully from hanging baskets and window boxes, or filling gaps between stepping stones. In rock gardens, it naturalizes readily and provides continuous color throughout the growing season, while its compact size makes it perfect for small-space gardens and urban containers.
