Stella d'Oro

Hemerocallis 'Stella de Oro'

perennialzones 3-9mid-June to fall (reblooming)

A compact reblooming daylily with bright golden-yellow flowers that bloom continuously from late spring through fall. This hardy perennial forms neat clumps of arching green foliage and produces trumpet-shaped blooms on sturdy stems. Known for its reliability and low maintenance requirements, making it perfect for borders and mass plantings.

Stella d'Oro is a compact, reblooming daylily cultivar renowned for its continuous production of bright golden-yellow trumpet-shaped flowers from late spring through fall. This hardy perennial has become one of the most popular landscape plants due to its exceptional reliability, low maintenance requirements, and ability to form neat, attractive clumps with arching green foliage.

Care

Summarized from the sources listed below (web research, 2026-11-02). Where sources disagree, the page says so.

What helps it thrive

Full sun gives the best bloom; part shade works but flowering drops in deep shade. Wants well-drained, organic-rich, slightly acidic soil. Fairly drought tolerant once established, but lower leaves yellow and flowering falls off if it stays too dry. Removing spent blooms keeps the rebloom going.

Growing season

Hardy zones 3-9. Reblooming: starts around mid-June and keeps flowering to fall if spent blooms are removed. Foliage dies back in fall (sometimes semi-evergreen).

Fall tasks

Cut old foliage back to the ground in fall (sources differ a little on timing and on how far back). Winter mulch is optional for established plants but helps new divisions (straw or shredded leaves). If planting in fall, do it at least 6 weeks before first frost.

Propagation

Division is the usual method. Seedlings will not match the parent, so cultivars do not come true from seed.

Dividing

Every 3-4 years (White Flower Farm suggests every 2 years for rebloomers). Timing: early spring or right after flowering; spring is preferred in cold-winter climates (Missouri Botanical Garden), and a late-summer split needs at least 6 weeks to re-establish before winter. Dig the clump and pry it apart (two spading forks back to back help). Keep young outer divisions with 2-3 fans of leaves and their roots, discard the tough old center, trim foliage to 6-8 inches, replant with the crown about 1 inch below the soil line and water thoroughly.

Research notes

Researched 2026-05-12.

Research (May 12, 2026)
Stella d'Oro (Hemerocallis 'Stella de Oro') represents a breakthrough in daylily breeding, developed by Walter Jablonski and introduced in 1975. Unlike traditional daylilies that bloom for a few weeks, this cultivar exhibits remarkable reblooming characteristics, producing waves of bright golden-yellow flowers continuously throughout the growing season. The compact plant typically reaches 10-12 inches in height with a similar spread, making it ideal for small gardens and formal landscapes where space is at a premium.

The plant's success stems from its exceptional adaptability and minimal care requirements. Stella d'Oro thrives in full sun to partial shade and tolerates a wide range of soil conditions, from clay to sandy soils, provided they have adequate drainage. Its drought tolerance once established, combined with resistance to most common pests and diseases, has made it a staple in low-maintenance landscaping. The cultivar performs well in USDA hardiness zones 3-9, demonstrating remarkable cold tolerance for a reblooming variety.

In landscape design, Stella d'Oro excels in mass plantings, border edges, and as groundcover due to its uniform growth habit and extended blooming period. The bright yellow flowers complement both cool and warm color schemes, while the neat foliage provides structure even when not in bloom. Its popularity has led to widespread commercial cultivation, making it readily available and affordable for home gardeners seeking reliable, long-blooming perennials.
